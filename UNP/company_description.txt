Union Pacific Corporation (UNP) is the parent company of Union Pacific Railroad, the largest railroad network in the United States. Founded in 1862 under the Pacific Railroad Act signed by President Abraham Lincoln, the company played a pivotal role in connecting the American frontier by completing the eastern leg of the First Transcontinental Railroad. Today, the company operates across 23 states in the western two-thirds of the country, maintaining a vast network of over 32,000 miles of track. Its business model centers on the efficient rail transportation of diverse freight, including agricultural products, automotive components, chemicals, coal, industrial materials, and intermodal containers. By linking major West Coast and Gulf Coast ports with eastern gateways and the Mexican border, Union Pacific serves as a critical artery for North American supply chains, facilitating domestic commerce and international trade through a highly integrated logistics infrastructure.
