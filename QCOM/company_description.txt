Qualcomm Incorporated, trading under the ticker QCOM, is a multinational semiconductor and telecommunications equipment company that designs and markets wireless telecommunications products and services. Founded in 1985 by Irwin Jacobs and a team of colleagues in San Diego, California, the company pioneered CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access) digital wireless technology, which laid the technical foundation for modern cellular communications. 

Over the decades, Qualcomm evolved from an R&D-focused startup into a global powerhouse in the mobile industry. While the company initially gained prominence for its pioneering work in 2G and 3G standards, it has since become a dominant developer of foundational technologies and system-on-chips for 4G and 5G networks. Today, Qualcomm’s business model centers around two primary pillars: Qualcomm CDMA Technologies, which develops and supplies integrated circuits and system software for smartphones, automotive applications, and the Internet of Things, and Qualcomm Technology Licensing, which manages an extensive patent portfolio covering vital mobile standards. By licensing its intellectual property to virtually every major handset manufacturer worldwide alongside its semiconductor sales, Qualcomm maintains a central role in the global mobile ecosystem and continues to expand into adjacent markets like computing and connected vehicles.
