Chevron Corporation, trading under the ticker CVX, is one of the world's largest integrated energy companies. Headquartered in San Ramon, California, Chevron operates across the entire petroleum and chemical supply chain, categorized primarily into upstream and downstream operations. Its upstream segment focuses on the exploration, development, and production of crude oil and natural gas. Its downstream activities encompass the refining of crude oil into petroleum products, manufacturing petrochemicals and lubricants, and marketing these products globally under brands such as Chevron, Texaco, and Caltex. Increasingly, the company is also investing in lower-carbon initiatives, including renewable fuels, hydrogen, and carbon capture technologies.

Chevron traces its roots to 1879 with the founding of the Pacific Coast Oil Company. It later became part of John D. Rockefeller’s Standard Oil trust, emerging as the Standard Oil Company of California (Socal) following the trust's 1911 antitrust dissolution. The modern enterprise was largely shaped by landmark consolidations, notably its 1984 acquisition of Gulf Oil—which led to the adoption of the Chevron name—and its 2001 merger with Texaco. Today, Chevron maintains vast global assets spanning North America, South America, Africa, Asia, and Australia.
