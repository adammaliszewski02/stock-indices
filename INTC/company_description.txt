Intel Corporation, traded under the ticker INTC, is a global leader in the design and manufacturing of semiconductor technologies. Founded in 1968 by technology pioneers Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore in Santa Clara, California, Intel revolutionized modern computing by introducing the world’s first commercial microprocessor, the Intel 4004, in 1971, ultimately establishing the x86 architecture as the standard for personal computing. 

Today, Intel's primary business centers on developing central processing units, chipsets, and integrated platforms for client computing, enterprise data centers, and cloud infrastructure, anchored by its well-known Core and Xeon product families. The company has also expanded its portfolio into graphics processing units, networking hardware, edge computing, and artificial intelligence accelerators. Crucially, Intel operates as an integrated device manufacturer, combining chip design with internal fabrication capabilities. Through its Intel Foundry division, the company is actively expanding its manufacturing footprint to produce advanced semiconductors both for its own proprietary products and as a commercial contract manufacturer for third-party global clients.
