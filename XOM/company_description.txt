Exxon Mobil Corporation (ticker: XOM) is a multinational energy corporation headquartered in Spring, Texas, and is one of the world's largest publicly traded energy companies and refiners. The company’s business operations are globally integrated, encompassing the exploration, extraction, transportation, and sale of crude oil and natural gas. Additionally, ExxonMobil manufactures and markets commodity petrochemicals, including olefins and polyolefins, alongside specialized performance products, and maintains a growing portfolio focused on lower-emission technologies such as carbon capture, hydrogen, and advanced biofuels. 

The company's origins trace back to John D. Rockefeller’s Standard Oil trust, founded in 1870. Following the landmark 1911 antitrust dissolution of Standard Oil, the enterprise split into several regional successors, most notably the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey (which later adopted the name Exxon) and the Standard Oil Company of New York (later known as Mobil). The modern corporation was formed in November 1999 through the historic merger of Exxon and Mobil Corporation, uniting two of the largest descendants of the original trust into a single global energy powerhouse.
