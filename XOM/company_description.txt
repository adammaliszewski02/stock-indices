Exxon Mobil Corporation (NYSE: XOM) is an American multinational energy giant headquartered in Spring, Texas, and one of the largest publicly traded oil and gas companies in the world. 

The company operates across the entire energy value chain through integrated business divisions. Its upstream segment explores for, produces, and markets crude oil and natural gas. The downstream operations refine petroleum into commercial fuels, asphalt, and lubricants, while its chemical division manufactures petrochemicals, plastics, and specialized resins. In recent years, ExxonMobil has also expanded into lower-emission initiatives, focusing on carbon capture and storage, hydrogen, and advanced biofuels.

Historically, the company traces its roots directly to John D. Rockefeller’s Standard Oil Company, established in 1870. Following the landmark 1911 Supreme Court antitrust breakup of the Standard Oil trust, two of its prominent successor entities—Standard Oil of New Jersey (which later became Exxon) and Standard Oil of New York (which evolved into Mobil)—grew into dominant global corporations. In 1999, the two companies reunited through an $81 billion merger, forming the modern Exxon Mobil Corporation. Today, the company remains a cornerstone of the global energy and manufacturing infrastructure.
