Exxon Mobil Corporation (XOM) is a premier American multinational energy corporation and one of the largest publicly traded companies in the world. Operating as an integrated oil and gas enterprise, the company engages in the exploration, production, transportation, and sale of crude oil, natural gas, and petroleum products. Additionally, ExxonMobil maintains a massive global chemical manufacturing business, producing high-performance plastics, synthetic rubbers, and specialized petrochemicals, while increasingly investing in lower-emissions technologies such as carbon capture and hydrogen. 

The corporation traces its direct lineage to Standard Oil, founded by John D. Rockefeller in 1870. Following the landmark 1911 antitrust dissolution of the Standard Oil trust, two of its primary successor entities emerged as Exxon (originally Standard Oil of New Jersey) and Mobil (originally Standard Oil of New York). These two corporate giants operated independently for decades before merging in 1999 to form ExxonMobil, creating a unified global powerhouse that has continuously adapted to evolving geopolitical landscapes, technological advancements, and the global transition toward sustainable energy sources.
