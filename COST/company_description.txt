Costco Wholesale Corporation, trading under the ticker COST, is an American multinational retail corporation that operates an international chain of membership-only warehouse clubs. Founded in 1983 by James Sinegal and Jeffrey Brotman in Seattle, Washington, the company's lineage also connects to Sol Price's pioneering Price Club, founded in 1976, which merged with Costco in 1993 before the combined entity adopted the Costco Wholesale name in 1999.

Costco’s business model relies on high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover achieved by offering a curated selection of merchandise at low profit margins. The company sells groceries, appliances, electronics, and apparel in bulk quantities to individual consumers and businesses who pay an annual membership fee. A significant driver of its value proposition is its private-label brand, Kirkland Signature, alongside ancillary operations including gas stations, pharmacies, optical centers, and food courts. By offsetting low product markups with recurring membership revenue and maintaining strict operational efficiencies, Costco has established itself as one of the largest and most resilient retailers globally.
