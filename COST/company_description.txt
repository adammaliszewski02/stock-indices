Costco Wholesale Corporation, trading under the ticker COST, operates an international chain of membership-only warehouse clubs that offer a wide selection of quality, nationally branded, and selected private-label merchandise at low prices. The company's business model focuses on driving high-volume sales through limited stock keeping units, allowing it to operate on low margins and pass significant savings along to its members. Founded in Seattle, Washington, in 1983 by James Sinegal and Jeffrey Brotman, the company opened its first warehouse that same year. It later expanded significantly through a 1993 merger with Price Club, a pioneer of the warehouse club concept founded in San Diego in 1976. Today, Costco generates the majority of its profits from annual membership fees rather than traditional retail markups, fostering deep customer loyalty and exceptional retention rates. Headquartered in Issaquah, Washington, the corporation has grown into one of the largest retailers in the world, operating hundreds of warehouses globally across North America, Asia, Europe, and Australia, while continually expanding its digital retail and delivery capabilities.
