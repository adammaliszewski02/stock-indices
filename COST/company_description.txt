Costco Wholesale Corporation, trading under the ticker COST, operates an international chain of membership-only big-box retail warehouse stores. The company’s business model focuses on offering a curated selection of quality brand-name and private-label merchandise, including groceries, electronics, apparel, and hardware, at significantly lower markups than traditional wholesalers or conventional retailers. Costco generates a substantial portion of its operating income from membership fees, which allows the company to price its inventory near cost to deliver maximum value to its members. 

The company's origins trace back to the opening of its first warehouse in Seattle, Washington, in 1983 under the guidance of founders James Sinegal and Jeffrey Brotman. A pivotal moment in its history occurred in 1993 through a merger with Price Club, a pioneering warehouse club founded in San Diego in 1976 by Sol Price. Following the merger, the company operated as Price/Costco before officially adopting the Costco Wholesale Corporation name in 1997. Today, Costco has evolved into one of the largest retailers in the world, renowned for its strong employee relations, efficient supply chain logistics, and high customer retention rates across hundreds of locations globally.
