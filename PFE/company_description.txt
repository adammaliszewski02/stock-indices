Pfizer Inc., traded under the ticker PFE, is a leading American multinational pharmaceutical and biotechnology corporation. Headquartered in New York City, the company discovers, develops, and manufactures a diverse portfolio of healthcare products, including innovative medicines, vaccines, and consumer healthcare items. 

Founded in 1849 by German-American cousins Charles Pfizer and Charles Erhart as a fine chemicals business in Brooklyn, the enterprise initially focused on producing parasecticides and expanding into citric acid production. Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, Pfizer transformed into a research-based pharmaceutical powerhouse, playing a pivotal role in the mass production of penicillin to support Allied forces during World War II. 

Throughout its modern history, Pfizer has driven significant medical breakthroughs, successfully commercializing globally recognized therapies and vaccines across diverse therapeutic areas such as immunology, oncology, cardiology, and neurology. Driven by robust internal research and strategic acquisitions, the company remains at the forefront of global public health innovation, most notably highlighted by its rapid development of a landmark mRNA-based vaccine in collaboration with BioNTech during the COVID-19 pandemic.
