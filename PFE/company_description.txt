Pfizer Inc., traded under the ticker PFE, is a leading American multinational pharmaceutical and biotechnology corporation. Headquartered in New York City, the company discovers, develops, and delivers a wide array of healthcare products, including innovative medicines and vaccines across immunology, oncology, cardiology, endocrinology, and neurology. 

Founded in 1849 by German-American cousins Charles Pfizer and Charles F. Erhart as a fine chemical business in Brooklyn, New York, the enterprise initially gained prominence by producing santonin, an antiparasitic agent. Over the subsequent decades, Pfizer evolved into a research-based pharmaceutical powerhouse. The company played a pivotal role in the mass production of penicillin to treat Allied soldiers during World War II, establishing its manufacturing capability and commitment to public health. 

Throughout its history, Pfizer has introduced numerous blockbuster medications that transformed global healthcare, culminating in recent decades with the rapid co-development of a breakthrough mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccine. Today, Pfizer continues to leverage cutting-edge science and global infrastructure to address some of the world's most challenging diseases.
