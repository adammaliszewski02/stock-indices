PepsiCo, Inc. (ticker PEP) is a multinational food, snack, and beverage corporation headquartered in Purchase, New York. The company manufactures, markets, and distributes a globally recognized portfolio of grain-based snacks, carbonated and non-carbonated beverages, and dairy products. Its enterprise encompasses iconic brands such as Pepsi-Cola, Quaker Oats, Gatorade, Frito-Lay, and Tropicana. 

The company's origins trace back to 1898, when pharmacist Caleb Bradham invented the carbonated soft drink "Pepsi-Cola" in New Bern, North Carolina. The modern corporate entity was formed through the historic 1965 merger of the Pepsi-Cola Company and Frito-Lay, Inc., a strategic combination that successfully unified major beverage and snack operations under a single umbrella. Over subsequent decades, PepsiCo expanded its global footprint and diversified its product offerings through key acquisitions, including Tropicana in 1998 and the Quaker Oats Company in 2001. Today, PepsiCo operates on a global scale, serving consumers in more than 200 countries and territories while driving sustainability initiatives through its pep+ (PepsiCo Positive) transformation framework, which focuses on sustainable agriculture, water stewardship, and product reformulation to meet evolving health and environmental standards.
