Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a prominent multinational semiconductor company specializing in the development of computer processors and related technologies for business and consumer markets. Founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and a group of colleagues, the company initially focused on logic chips before entering the memory chip market in the 1970s and launching its first proprietary processor, the Am9080, in 1975. Over the decades, AMD evolved into a primary competitor to Intel in the central processing unit (CPU) sector. A major milestone in its corporate history occurred in 2006 with the acquisition of ATI Technologies, which significantly expanded AMD’s portfolio into graphics processing units (GPUs). Today, AMD is a fabless semiconductor designer, utilizing outsourced manufacturing to produce high-performance CPUs, GPUs, and adaptive computing solutions. Its core offerings power a vast array of modern computing environments, ranging from personal computers and gaming consoles like the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S to enterprise data centers and cutting-edge artificial intelligence infrastructure.
