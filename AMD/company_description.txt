Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a leading American multinational semiconductor company that designs and develops high-performance computing and graphics solutions. Founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues, AMD initially produced logic chips before expanding into memory and microprocessors, eventually establishing itself as the primary rival to Intel in the x86 CPU market. In 2009, the company shifted to a fabless model by spinning off its manufacturing arm, GlobalFoundries, to focus purely on chip architecture and design.

Today, AMD’s business encompasses central processing units (CPUs), graphics processing units (GPUs), and adaptive computing solutions. Its prominent product lines include Ryzen processors for personal computing, Radeon graphics cards for gaming, and EPYC processors tailored for cloud computing and enterprise data centers. AMD also engineers semi-custom system-on-chips that power leading gaming consoles, including the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S. Following its strategic acquisition of Xilinx in 2022, AMD expanded substantially into field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) and embedded systems, strengthening its capabilities in artificial intelligence, telecommunications, and industrial automation.
