Texas Instruments Incorporated (TXN) is a global semiconductor manufacturer that designs, innovates, manufactures, and sells analog and embedded processing semiconductors for industrial, automotive, personal electronics, communications equipment, and enterprise systems markets. The company leverages a vast intellectual property portfolio to focus on high-margin, long-lifecycle products. 

Founded in 1930 as Geophysical Service Incorporated, the firm initially provided seismic data analysis for the petroleum industry before entering the defense electronics sector. In 1951, it was renamed General Instrument, and shortly thereafter adopted its current name. A pivotal moment in technological history occurred at the company in 1958 when engineer Jack Kilby invented the integrated circuit, fundamentally transforming global electronics. During the mid-20th century, Texas Instruments became a dominant consumer electronics pioneer, introducing the first commercial silicon transistor in 1954 and launching iconic handheld calculators in the 1970s. Over subsequent decades, the company strategically divested its defense, consumer, and personal computer segments—including the sale of its mobile OMAP processor business—to concentrate exclusively on analog and embedded semiconductors. Today, headquartered in Dallas, Texas, TXN remains an industry leader committed to manufacturing efficiency through internal 300-millimeter wafer production.
