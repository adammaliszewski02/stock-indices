Texas Instruments Incorporated (TXN) is a global semiconductor design and manufacturing company that develops analog chips and embedded processors for industrial, automotive, personal electronics, communications equipment, and enterprise systems markets. Headquartered in Dallas, Texas, the company operates design, manufacturing, and sales facilities worldwide, leveraging its proprietary analog and digital signal processing technology. 

Founded in 1930 as Geophysical Service Inc., the firm initially focused on seismic exploration for the oil and gas industry before entering the defense electronics sector and adopting its current name in 1951. A pioneer in microelectronics, Texas Instruments invented the integrated circuit in 1958 and introduced the first commercial silicon transistor in 1954. Over subsequent decades, the company evolved from a diverse conglomerate—which previously manufactured consumer calculators and defense systems—into a pure-play semiconductor powerhouse following the 1990s and 2000s divestment of its defense and memory businesses. Today, TXN differentiates itself through its extensive proprietary manufacturing capacity, long-lifecycle product portfolios, and strategic focus on high-growth automotive and industrial applications.
