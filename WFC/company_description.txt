Wells Fargo & Company, trading under the stock ticker WFC, is a prominent American multinational financial services company holding a massive presence in global banking. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the institution traces its origins to 1852, when it was founded by Henry Wells and William Fargo to provide express delivery and banking services during the California Gold Rush. Over the decades, through strategic mergers and organic growth—most notably its pivotal 2008 acquisition of Charlotte-based Wachovia—the firm evolved into one of the "Big Four" banks in the United States. Today, Wells Fargo operates a diversified business model serving millions of consumers, small businesses, and major corporations. Its core operations encompass community banking, commercial banking, corporate and investment banking, as well as wealth and investment management. The institution is widely recognized for its extensive brick-and-mortar branch and ATM network across the nation, alongside a continually expanding suite of digital financial platforms designed to facilitate lending, mortgage servicing, asset management, and everyday transactional banking.
