Wells Fargo & Company (ticker: WFC) is a leading American multinational financial services corporation and one of the "Big Four" banks in the United States. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the company provides a comprehensive suite of banking, investment, mortgage, and consumer and commercial finance products and services to individuals, businesses, and institutions globally. 

The enterprise traces its origins back to 1852, when Henry Wells and William G. Fargo founded the company in New York to capitalize on the booming California gold rush by offering express delivery and banking services. Over the decades, it expanded aggressively through a series of strategic mergers and acquisitions, most notably its transformative 1998 combination with Minneapolis-based Norwest Corporation—which retained the globally recognized Wells Fargo name—and its 2008 acquisition of Charlotte-based Wachovia during the global financial crisis. Today, Wells Fargo operates thousands of retail branches and administrative offices across North America, serving tens of millions of customers as a foundational pillar of the modern American banking system.
