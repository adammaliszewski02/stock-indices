Wells Fargo & Company, trading under the ticker WFC, is a prominent American multinational financial services corporation and one of the largest banks in the United States by assets. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the company traces its origins back to 1852, when Henry Wells and William Fargo founded an enterprise to provide express and banking services during the California Gold Rush. Over the decades, Wells Fargo expanded significantly through strategic mergers and acquisitions, most notably its transformative 1998 combination with Minneapolis-based Norwest Corporation and its 2008 acquisition of Charlotte-based Wachovia during the global financial crisis. Today, the corporation operates a diversified business model serving tens of millions of customers globally. Its core operations are organized into four primary business segments: Consumer Lending; Commercial Banking; Corporate and Investment Banking; and Wealth and Investment Management. Wells Fargo provides a comprehensive suite of financial products and services, including retail and commercial banking, mortgages, credit cards, investment advisory services, and treasury management, positioning itself as a cornerstone of the modern American banking system.
