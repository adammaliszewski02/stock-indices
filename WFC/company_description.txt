Wells Fargo & Company, traded under the ticker WFC on the New York Stock Exchange, is a major American multinational financial services corporation and one of the country's "Big Four" banks. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the firm operates through four primary segments: Consumer Banking and Lending, Commercial Banking, Corporate and Investment Banking, and Wealth and Investment Management. It provides traditional deposit accounts, credit cards, mortgages, commercial financing, and advisory services to individuals, small businesses, and large institutions.

Founded in 1852 by Henry Wells and William G. Fargo, the company initially established express delivery and banking operations to capitalize on the California Gold Rush. It gained fame for utilizing six-horse stagecoaches to securely transport gold, mail, and currency across the western frontier, establishing a network that facilitated Western expansion. Over the subsequent decades, through numerous mergers—most notably its 1998 combination with Norwest Corporation and its 2008 acquisition of Wachovia—Wells Fargo transitioned into a nationwide banking network. Today, it remains one of the largest financial institutions and mortgage originators in the United States.
