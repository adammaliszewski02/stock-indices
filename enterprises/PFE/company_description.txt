Pfizer Inc., traded under the ticker PFE, is a leading American multinational pharmaceutical and biotechnology corporation. Headquartered in New York City, the company discovers, develops, and manufactures a wide range of healthcare products, including innovative medicines, vaccines, and consumer healthcare items. Founded in 1849 by cousins Charles Pfizer and Charles Erhart as a fine chemical business in Brooklyn, New York, the enterprise initially focused on producing parasitol and expanded rapidly during the American Civil War by supplying essential painkillers and disinfectants to the Union Army. Over the decades, Pfizer evolved from a chemical manufacturer into a research-driven pharmaceutical powerhouse, pioneering the mass production of penicillin during World War II and subsequently introducing numerous blockbuster therapies. In the modern era, Pfizer remains at the forefront of global medical innovation, supported by extensive research and development operations dedicated to addressing complex diseases across oncology, immunology, cardiology, and rare conditions.
