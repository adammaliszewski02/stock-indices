Wells Fargo & Company (ticker: WFC) is a leading American multinational financial services corporation and one of the "Big Four" banks in the United States. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the company provides a comprehensive suite of banking, investment, mortgage, and consumer and commercial finance products and services to individuals, businesses, and institutions. 

Tracing its roots back to 1852, when Henry Wells and William G. Fargo founded the enterprise in New York to capitalize on the California Gold Rush, the institution originally operated as both an express delivery and banking service, famously utilizing stagecoaches for secure transport. Over the decades, it evolved through numerous mergers and acquisitions—most notably its combination with Minneapolis-based Norwest Corporation in 1998 and the landmark 2008 acquisition of Wachovia during the global financial crisis. Today, Wells Fargo operates a vast network of branches and digital platforms, serving tens of millions of customers globally while maintaining a foundational role in the American economic infrastructure.
