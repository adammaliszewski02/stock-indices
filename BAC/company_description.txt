Bank of America Corporation (BAC) is one of the world's leading financial institutions, providing a comprehensive range of banking, investing, asset management, and other financial and risk-management products and services. Headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina, the company serves individual consumers, small and middle-market businesses, corporations, and governments through a robust digital platform and a vast network of financial centers across the United States. 

The institution traces its roots to 1904, when Amadeo Peter Giannini founded the Bank of Italy in San Francisco to serve immigrants and working-class people denied service by traditional banks. Following the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, Giannini famously resumed operations immediately from a makeshift desk, fostering the trust that fueled rapid expansion. Through a series of major mergers and acquisitions over the decades—most notably the 1998 merger with NationsBank, which adopted the Bank of America name, and the 2008 acquisition of Merrill Lynch during the global financial crisis—the firm evolved into a universal banking powerhouse. Today, BAC operates as a publicly traded corporation on the New York Stock Exchange, recognized globally for its systemic importance and extensive market reach.
