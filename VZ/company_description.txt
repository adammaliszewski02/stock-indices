Verizon Communications Inc., trading under the stock ticker VZ, is a leading American multinational telecommunications conglomerate and one of the world's largest providers of technology, communications, information, and entertainment products and services. Headquartered in New York City, the company traces its origins back to the 1984 breakup of AT&T, known as the "Baby Bells," through the formation of Bell Atlantic Corporation. Bell Atlantic subsequently merged with GTE Corporation in 2000, at which time the combined entity was renamed Verizon, a portmanteau of "veritas" (truth) and "horizon." Today, Verizon operates primarily through two segments: Verizon Consumer Group and Verizon Business. The company delivers wireless and wireline voice and data services across its advanced nationwide networks, providing high-speed internet, television, and security solutions to millions of consumers, businesses, and government agencies.
