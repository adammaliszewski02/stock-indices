Verizon Communications Inc., trading under the stock ticker VZ, is a leading American multinational telecommunications conglomerate and one of the world's largest providers of technology, communications, information, and entertainment products and services. Headquartered in New York City, the company traces its origins back to the 1984 breakup of AT&T, which created Bell Atlantic, one of the original Regional Bell Operating Companies. Bell Atlantic subsequently merged with GTE in 2000, formally adopting the name Verizon—a portmanteau of "veritas" (Latin for truth) and "horizon." Today, Verizon operates primarily through two segments: Verizon Consumer Group and Verizon Business. The company delivers wireless voice and data services across its expansive nationwide networks, alongside high-speed fiber-optic broadband services through its Fios infrastructure. Over the decades, Verizon has evolved from a traditional regional telephone utility into a dominant wireless carrier and digital media provider, continuously investing in next-generation network architecture, including the nationwide deployment of 5G technology.
