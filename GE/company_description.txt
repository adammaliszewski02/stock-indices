General Electric Company (GE), identified by the ticker GE, is a globally recognized American multinational corporation with a rich history spanning over a century. Founded in 1892 through the merger of Edison General Electric Company and Thomson-Houston Electric Company, GE historically operated as a diversified industrial conglomerate. Over decades, the company drove technological innovation, shaping various sectors including power generation, aviation, healthcare, and renewable energy, while also operating a major financial services arm. 

In recent years, GE underwent a profound corporate transformation. The company strategically divested its healthcare and major appliance divisions and significantly restructured its financial services. Continuing this historic evolution, GE completed a definitive three-way corporate split, dividing its remaining operations into independent, publicly traded entities: GE Aerospace, GE Vernova (focused on the energy transition), and GE HealthCare. Today, GE Aerospace continues the legacy of the original ticker, focusing on commercial and military jet engines and aviation systems, while the broader historical entity's various foundational businesses operate as standalone market leaders in their respective industries.
